# AUDIENCE PROFILE SKILL — THE INTENTIONAL TEA
## Who Tiara Is Writing For

This skill covers two distinct audiences. Check which one a piece of writing is actually for before applying persona detail — using the personal-side audience's language on Business Operations copy (or vice versa) is a fast way to sound like it was written for someone else.

**How the two relate:** The brand's internal philosophy (see business-profile skill) is that Tiara helps one kind of person align both their life and their business. That's true as a worldview. It is not the same claim as "these are the same buyer." In practice, someone buying a $25 digital planner and someone hiring Tiara for a $2,500 infrastructure build are very often different people, arriving through different doors, with different vocabularies and different objections. Write to the audience actually in front of you.

# PART ONE — THE PERSONAL-SIDE AUDIENCE
## Digital Planning, Life Reset, Astrology Report

### THE CORE INSIGHT — READ THIS FIRST

The audience is not defined by demographics, aesthetics, or career stage. It is defined by emotional season.

The common denominator across every person this brand reaches is identity transition — specifically, people who are in the process of rebuilding their life intentionally instead of automatically.

They share one thing: "Something about my current life no longer fits, and I want to rebuild in a way that actually feels aligned to me — not the way I was told it should look."

This is why the brand can speak to:

- Someone who just got laid off from a corporate job they were good at
- Someone who just had a baby and is trying to hold onto her career identity
- Someone who graduated college but can't get the job she was supposed to get
- Someone who didn't go to college but is figuring out what she actually wants to build
- Someone who has everything "on paper" and still feels disconnected
- Someone who had a career change and is still figuring out if it was the right one
- Someone about to step into a new life stage (college, marriage, a move) and wants to arrive intentionally
- Someone who has been in survival mode for so long she doesn't know who she is outside of it

Different circumstances. Same emotional season. Same core need.

### DEMOGRAPHIC OVERVIEW

- Age: 24-38 (core), with some younger (18-23) and older (39-45)
- Primarily Black women, with strong resonance among women of color broadly
- Education: College-educated or highly self-educated — not defined by credentials
- Income: Mid to high income bracket, or actively curating that lifestyle
- Location: Urban or suburban; cities with wellness/creative communities (Atlanta, LA, DC, Brooklyn, Houston, Charlotte, Austin, and others)

These are descriptors, not the definition. The emotional profile below is what actually matters.

### EMOTIONAL PROFILE — THE ACTUAL DEFINITION

**Who she is on the inside:** Emotionally intelligent, self-aware, already doing the work — therapy, journaling, reading, podcasts. Not new to personal development. Not a beginner.

She values softness. She's spent years in environments that required her to be useful, consistent, and legible to people who couldn't hold her complexity — corporate jobs, relationships, family dynamics. She's done performing. She wants systems that don't ask her to pretend she's a machine.

She's the therapist friend — the support system for everyone around her, learning slowly to put herself first, knowing she needs it, not always knowing how without guilt.

She's spiritual but not performatively so. She knows her birth chart, probably has an opinion on human design, pulls tarot cards or finds them interesting even without a deck. She doesn't want to be talked to like she needs Mercury retrograde explained.

She's multi-passionate and has probably been told to niche down — career, content, interests. She resists this, correctly.

She desires spaces where she doesn't need to explain or perform her identity — especially as a Black woman used to being the only one in wellness spaces, the one who code-switches, the one told she's "too much" or "too quiet" or "too sensitive."

**What transition looks like for her, specifically — real persona examples (not marketing archetypes, real people Tiara knows):**

- **Tiara's own version:** Corporate layoff — fun-employed season — intentional business build. Already did the work. The Reset helped her document and structure a transformation already in progress.
- **Sierra's version:** Married, new baby, entry-level career slowly progressing. Trying to maintain career identity while navigating new motherhood. Not unhappy — stalled, wondering if this is what she wanted or just what made sense.
- **Rain's version:** College graduate, can't get the job the degree was supposed to unlock. Holding pattern that feels like failure though it isn't. Asking whether to move cities, start over, force a different option.
- **The sister's version:** Didn't go the traditional route. Worked her way up, found something she's good at, starting to think about building something of her own. Doesn't need to be told to dream — needs a system for what she's already dreaming about.
- **Victoria's version:** Career change into mortgages/real estate, young mom, just married. Multi-transition. Trying to figure out who she is across all of it at once.
- **Marilyn's version:** Engaged, great job — better than the last one — but still asking "is this what I want?" at a relatively settled point. Not crisis. Quiet questioning.
- **Younger sister's version:** About to graduate high school, stepping into college, wants to arrive intentionally. Wants self-development work but therapy isn't accessible yet.
- **The "had it on paper" version:** Followed the path — school, grades, job, relationship, maybe homeownership. Technically successful. Internally disconnected. Asking whether she's living her own life or the life she was handed.

### ENTRY POINTS

Healing burnout, soft living and aesthetic organization, nervous system regulation, rebuilding after toxic environments, intentional routines without self-punishment, feminine energy and soft authority, spirituality and self-trust, reinvention after collapse or transition, digital planning and iPad organization, content creation and building something, emotional clarity and self-understanding, manifestation and alignment practices.

All doors into the same emotional universe: *I'm in a transition and I want to move through it intentionally.*

### WORLDVIEW

**What she believes:** Healing is real work, not a trend. She's capable of building a good life — she just needs the right architecture. Her sensitivity and emotionality are strengths. Structure doesn't have to feel like a cage. She deserves a life that moves at her pace. The path she was handed probably wasn't built for someone like her.

**What she's tired of:** Generic wellness content that performs healing instead of facilitating it. Brands that see "Black women in wellness" as a niche to market to rather than a reality to build for. Productivity advice that assumes she's a machine. Spiritual content that's all vibe, no substance. Having to explain herself in spaces where everyone looks the same. Motivational content that asks her to hype herself up instead of helping her figure it out. Products that promise transformation and deliver aesthetic.

**What she responds to:** Specificity — language that names exactly what she's feeling without over-explaining it. Honesty about the messy, non-linear parts. Structure that acknowledges her capacity varies. Content that treats her intelligence as a given. Products built for her life as it actually is.

### RESISTANCE PATTERNS

**On digital products:** "I already have a planner I don't use." "I'll buy this and not actually do it." "I don't want to spend $55 on something that might not stick." "I've tried journaling. It didn't fix anything."

**On self-development content:** "I already know most of this stuff." "This is going to be surface-level." "This doesn't actually apply to my specific situation." "Everything in this space sounds the same."

**On investing in herself:** "I should be putting this money somewhere else." "I don't have the time to actually use this." "I'll start when things calm down." (Things never calm down.)

**What breaks through:** Specificity that makes her feel this was built for someone exactly like her. Proof the creator has actually lived this, not just studied it. A price point that doesn't feel like a commitment to something uncertain. Free entry points that let her experience quality before buying. No urgency language — she finds it manipulative and it loses her trust immediately.

### RELATIONSHIP TO KEY BRAND THEMES

**Ambition:** Ambitious but not hustle-culture. Wants a well-resourced life financially, emotionally, spiritually — without grinding for it, without performing productivity at the expense of her nervous system.

**Softness:** Protective of her peace. Choosing softness as a deliberate design decision, not a default or retreat. Doesn't want to be told softness and ambition are opposites.

**Burnout:** Many entry points lead back to burnout in some form — corporate, over-giving in relationships, trying to be everything for everyone. Not necessarily in crisis; often in recovery or prevention mode.

**Visibility:** May have a complicated relationship with being seen — building something but uncertain how visible she wants to be.

**Healing and performance:** Suspicious of performative healing. Knows the difference between processing and posting about processing. Wants the real thing, and can tell the difference.

### BEFORE AND AFTER

**Before:** In motion but not necessarily moving in the direction she wants. Has the tools — planners, journals, apps, saved posts, downloaded ebooks she hasn't read. Has the intention. Doesn't have a system that integrates intention with structure. Feels productive and still behind. Keeps starting and stopping.

**After:** Has language for patterns she used to just feel. Has a system that moves with her instead of against her. Returns to the tools when seasons shift instead of abandoning them. Makes decisions with more clarity, less second-guessing. Not dramatically "transformed" — more herself. That's the difference.

### FIVE READER PERSONAS

1. **The Corporate Reset** — mid-to-late career, high-performing, hit a wall. Often still in the job, fine on paper, disconnected internally. Entry: Life Reset, Astrology Report.
2. **The Transition Navigator** — active life transition (motherhood, new city, new relationship status, new career). Not falling apart — navigating. Entry: Digital Planner, Life Reset.
3. **The Stalled Starter** — hasn't landed the version of her life she was working toward. Energy and vision, not clarity or direction. Entry: Ebook Bundle, Life Reset, Essential Planning Templates.
4. **The Quiet Builder** — has figured out what she wants, or is close. Building something. Doesn't need to be sold the dream, needs infrastructure. Probably already uses GoodNotes. Entry: Digital Planner, Financial Planner, The Intentional Collection.
5. **The Self-Development Seeker** — younger or early in her journey, drawn to astrology/human design/journaling, hasn't found a brand that isn't too mystical or too corporate. Entry: Ebook Bundle, Q&A content, Stickers.

### HOW TO WRITE FOR HER

- **Open with her experience, not the product.** She wants to be seen, not described at.
- **Name specific emotions and situations.** Not "feeling overwhelmed" — feeling productive and still somehow behind.
- **Trust her intelligence.** Don't over-explain, don't define terms she knows, don't hedge into balance when she wants a clear position.
- **Don't perform healing at her.** She knows the difference between someone who's lived something and someone who's read about it.
- **Let her decide on her own timeline.** No urgency tactics.

# PART TWO — THE BUSINESS OPERATIONS AUDIENCE
## Small Business Owners Hiring for Systems and Infrastructure

**A note on depth:** This section is necessarily thinner than Part One. The personal-side audience has years of direct research, named real personas, and documented resistance patterns behind it. The Business Operations audience is newer and less mapped — inferred from the live offer copy, the "who this is for" and "not a fit if" language on the Work With Me page, and the actual first clients. Treat this as a working profile to sharpen with real client data over time, not a finished document to the same depth as Part One.

### THE CORE INSIGHT

This is not the same person as the digital-planning buyer, even though the same underlying brand philosophy (alignment, fit, structure) applies to both. This is a small business owner — often service-based, often solo or near-solo — who already has a working business but is running it manually, with a backend that hasn't kept pace with the business itself.

The shared thread across every Business Operations client: *"My business already works. I already have real customers and real momentum. What I don't have is the structure underneath it, and I'm the one holding it together by hand."*

### WHO SHE (OR HE) IS

- Runs a real, established service business — not a startup idea, not pre-revenue
- Good at the actual service they provide; not necessarily good at, or interested in, the operational backend
- Currently running on some combination of: DMs and phone calls instead of a booking system, manual client tracking, scattered tools that don't talk to each other, inconsistent or no digital presence
- Has hit a point where the manual version of running things is costing real time, real money, or real client experience — not a hypothetical future problem, a current one
- May be a solo service provider, a creative, a consultant, or a founder running more than one brand, location, or revenue stream

**Confirmed real examples (not personas — actual first clients):** A family-run snowball stand. Her father's business. Referral clients are now entering the pipeline beyond family.

### WHAT SHE'S TIRED OF

- Web designers who hand over a beautiful site with no plan for what happens when leads actually come in
- Operations people who can't design their way out of a template
- Being the only person who can answer every question in the business — that's not a discipline problem, it's a documentation problem
- DIY tutorials that assume time and technical patience she doesn't have
- Generic "one size fits all" packages that don't reflect how her specific business actually runs

### WHAT SHE RESPONDS TO

- Someone who does both design and backend systems together, not one or the other
- Being told plainly what's broken and what to fix first, without a sales pitch attached (this is exactly what the Clarity & Audit offer is built to deliver)
- Proof of real, specific work — not vague "results," an actual walkthrough of what was built and why
- A scoped quote instead of a generic package — she wants pricing that reflects her actual complexity, not a one-size menu
- Confidence that she'll understand what was built, not just receive it — the Loom-walkthrough handoff exists because "I don't understand my own systems" is a real fear

### WHO THIS IS NOT FOR

- Anyone who needs something built overnight with no planning phase
- Anyone who wants a template handed over and called a system
- Anyone not ready to share access, answer intake questions, or be reachable during the build
- Anyone looking for ongoing day-to-day admin management (that's a VA, not a systems build)

### ENTRY POINTS

Primarily inquiry-driven through the Work With Me page rather than the broad emotional-resonance entry points of the personal side. Referral (family, then word of mouth) has been the actual growth channel so far — though Pinterest, the blog's "Building Intentionally" pillar, and LinkedIn (if activated) are the intended future channels.

### HOW TO WRITE FOR HER

- **Lead with what's already working, not what's broken.** Don't open by diagnosing failure.
- **Name the specific operational friction, not a vague pain point.** Not "your business feels chaotic" — "you're the only one who can answer every question," "clients are falling through the cracks between a DM and a booking."
- **Credentials matter more here than on the personal side.** The Certified PM background, the enterprise-scale systems experience — this audience wants to know competence is real before they hand over logins and passwords.
- **No spiritual or astrology language.** Keep Business Operations copy in the Business Alignment register — structure, fit, friction, systems — not the fuller Intentional Living vocabulary.
- **Confidence over persuasion.** The tone should read like an assessment being offered, not a sale being made.
